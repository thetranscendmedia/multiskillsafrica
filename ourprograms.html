What We Teach

Five hands-on modules. One goal: skills that last a lifetime.

MSTP's training curriculum gives students practical, marketable abilities they can use in school, at home, and in their communities. Every module is hands-on, contextually relevant, and built on the belief that learning is most powerful when you can see, touch, and build the results.

Wood, Electronics & Technology (WET)

Description: Students learn to measure, cut, and finish wooden items - developing precision, patience, and craftmanship. along side woodwork, they explore simple electronic circuits, components, and safety practices.

What students gain: Identify tools and materials, construct basic wooden objects, and understand fundamental electronic principles.

Career pathways: Carpentry, furniture-making, electrical work, and technology repair.

Electrical & Electronics (E&E)

Description: Students go deeper into electrical systems—safety, wiring, switches, and simple installation practices. They gain hands-on experience with components and tools used in homes, schools, and businesses across Nigeria.

What students gain: Electrical safety, basic wiring, circuit understanding, and component identification.

Career pathways: Electrical installation, electronics repair, energy-related trades.

Garden, Nutrition, Agriculture & Trade (GNAT)

Description: Students connect to land and food systems—soil prep, planting, irrigation, harvesting. Nutrition education links food to health and well-being. The Trade Component introduces market skills and cooperative principles.

What students gain: Grow food, reduce household hunger, and participate in local trade.

Career pathways: Small-scale farming, market gardening, agri-cooperatives, nutrition advising.

Food Processing

Description: Students transform raw agricultural produce into marketable food products. Techniques include fermentation, drying, milling, and packaging—using methods appropriate to both home and small business production.

What students gain: Food preservation, product development, basic food business knowledge.

Career pathways: Agro-processing enterprises, food retail, nutrition businesses.

Introduction to Entrepreneurship

Description: Students learn to think like business owners: identify opportunities, write a simple business plan, understand customers, manage money, and present ideas confidently. Concepts covered include value creation, profit, reinvestment, and community impact.

Culmination: The MSTP Trade Fair—students display and sell products they have made, earning real income for the first time.

Career pathways: Self-employment, small business ownership, enterprise development.
